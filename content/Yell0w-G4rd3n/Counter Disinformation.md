The digital age has brought about monumental shifts in how we interact with the world around us. In recent decades, rapid technological transformations have redefined communication, access to knowledge, and how information is shared. Today, with a smartphone in hand, individuals have access to the vast wealth of human knowledge, and news can spread across the globe in mere seconds. While these advancements have opened new avenues for education, social connection, and information dissemination, they have also introduced significant challenges, particularly when it comes to the spread of misinformation and disinformation.

## Maximizing Access to Information

One of the primary goals in the fight against disinformation is to ensure that the public has easy access to accurate and trustworthy information. The internet has revolutionised how we consume news and knowledge, but this vast sea of data can sometimes drown out factual information in favor of sensationalised or misleading content. To counter this, it is crucial to develop strategies that enhance the accessibility of factual, evidence-based information.

Governments, educational institutions, and media organisations must collaborate to create reliable platforms and resources that can be easily accessed by the general public. Whether through dedicated websites, social media campaigns, or partnerships with existing platforms, the goal should be to ensure that truth has a fighting chance in the battle for attention.

For the younger generation, this means making sure that information is not only accessible but also presented in formats that resonate with their preferred modes of communication—videos, infographics, podcasts, and more. By utilising these tools, we can ensure that educational content is engaging and impactful, encouraging young people to seek out trustworthy sources and verify the accuracy of the information they encounter.

## Promoting Digital Literacy

Alongside increasing access to factual information, promoting digital literacy is a key strategy in combating disinformation. The rise of the digital era has meant that virtually everyone—regardless of age—must now be digitally literate to some degree. 

This issue is especially pressing for the aging population, who may not have grown up with the internet and may lack the necessary skills to identify disinformation. Organisations, particularly those in the non-profit and public sectors, must step in to bridge this gap by offering digital literacy programs tailored to these groups. Workshops that teach basic skills—such as how to use the internet safely, how to identify reputable sources, and how to protect oneself from digital threats—are essential to empowering older individuals to navigate the digital world with confidence and security.

## Working with Tech Companies

To further combat disinformation, collaboration with tech companies is essential. The role of tech companies in the spread of information is undeniable, and their platforms have become the primary vehicles through which news and ideas circulate. While many tech giants have taken steps to address disinformation—by implementing fact-checking features, limiting harmful content, or taking down misleading ads—the efforts are still insufficient.

Rather than taking a reactive approach, we need a more proactive and cooperative effort to tackle disinformation at its roots. Governments, civil society organizations, and media outlets should see collective evidence-based, factual content. Tech companies also have a responsibility to ensure that their platforms are not being exploited to spread harmful misinformation. We need to design policies and tools that ensure harmful disinformation does not overshadow reliable, evidence-based information.

By working together, stakeholders can create an environment where trust in digital platforms is restored, and information that is factual, transparent, and helpful is prioritized over sensationalism and lies.

## Closing thoughts

As the global political landscape continues to evolve at an unprecedented pace, the threat posed by state-funded actors seeking to destabilise communities through disinformation has become an urgent and significant challenge.These actors are manipulating public opinion, sow division, and undermine trust in institutions. Whether through social media, news outlets, or even covert online campaigns, the ability to influence the narratives of entire populations has become a powerful tool in the hands of those looking to disrupt the social order.

It is a global phenomenon, affecting democratic processes, international relations, and the fabric of societies. The spread of disinformation has the potential endanger the general public.

To counter this threat, it is we act swiftly to create and implement policies that address disinformation at its roots. This cannot be a reactive effort; we need to take proactive measures to identify and combat disinformation campaigns in their earliest stages. By building early detection systems and implementing robust strategies to counter harmful content, we can reduce the impact of these campaigns.

Furthermore, state-sponsored disinformation campaigns are becoming more sophisticated, it is essential to ensure that civilians are empowered to form their own narratives. This requires an emphasis on evidence-based information that allows people to critically evaluate the messages they encounter and make informed decisions. By promoting media literacy and providing access to factual, transparent, and diverse sources of information, we can give individuals the tools they need to resist manipulation and develop their own understanding of complex issues.

In the face of this growing threat, we must take a stand. The time to act is now.
